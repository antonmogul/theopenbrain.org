# Chapter timeline (OPENBRAIN-128)

The bar docked at the bottom of every chapter. It replaced the 2px reading-progress line along the top of `ReaderTopBar`.

## What it shows

The chapter as bars, SoundCloud-waveform style. There is one bar for each thing the reader passes, in reading order: a prose paragraph, a widget, or a break video or break section. Bars are equal width. A paragraph's bar height is its word count, measured against the chapter's 95th-percentile paragraph. Widgets and breaks are full-height hatched "block" bars, so they read as objects rather than text. Sections are separated by a small gap.

- **Read and unread.** Bars before the reading line (40% down the viewport) are filled in the chapter colour (`--color-chapter`). The bar being read is partly filled, and the rest are grey. In the light theme `perc` and `deve` fill with `--color-chapter-deep` instead: their main step is about as light as the unread grey (1.08:1 and 1.06:1), and the deep step keeps the two apart (1.65 and 1.71:1). Every other ramp, and every ramp in the dark theme, is past 1.9:1. Magenta (`--color-accent`) is only the fallback for when no chapter ramp is set (`html[data-chapter]` is missing).
- **Lanes.** These sit under the bars once the dock opens:
  - content: widget ■, figure ● (paragraph animation, image or full-screen figure), video ▶ (paragraph video, break video) and break ◆;
  - "you", for signed-in readers: a dot for each of your highlights in its colour, and ✎ for notes;
  - trending, for everyone: a cap above a bar whose passage readers have shared.
- **Three states.**
  - _Rest_ is a 20px strip with the `NN%` read label.
  - _Peek_ is 104px tall. It opens on hover over the dock itself, on keyboard focus, or on a tap on touch. It adds section labels, the lanes, a preview card for the bar under the pointer (section, opening line, widget card with thumbnail, your highlights and notes, trending) and the "Chapter map" button. The card is capped to the viewport above the open dock and scrolls if it is taller; under 520px of viewport height it drops the widget thumbnail. Excerpts leave out citation and footnote superscripts.
  - _Map_ is a full-screen dialog, loaded on first open (its own chunk). It has a large copy of the bars, then the chapter grouped by section: subsections, widgets, figures, your highlights and notes, and the section's top three trending passages. Each entry is a button that closes the map and jumps there. A section or subsection entry lands on its heading. From 1024px a section whose first paragraph runs a figure transition opens with a 200vh `.section-transition-spacer`; the jump lands past it, on the section's number badge. Focus starts on the close button and Tab wraps inside the dialog. Escape, or the close button from the keyboard, puts focus back on the map button in the peek. A click or tap on the close button folds the dock and puts focus on the slider without peeking; a jump from the map folds it too.
- **Input.**
  - Click or tap a bar to jump to it. On touch, a tap on the resting strip opens the peek, the first tap on a bar selects it, and a second tap (or "Go here") jumps. A tap elsewhere or a scroll of more than 48px folds it.
  - The bar area is a `role="slider"`. Left and Right move a cursor bar, PageUp and PageDown move between sections, Home and End go to either end, Enter jumps and Escape closes. A polite live region reads the cursor's bar. A mouse press or a tap never focuses the slider, only the keyboard does, so a click doesn't leave it holding the page's scroll keys.
  - Every timeline and map jump moves focus to where it lands, so Tab and a screen reader carry on from there (a keyboard jump folds the dock as focus leaves it). An item jump also flashes the paragraph (`.ob-flash`). An element that can't take focus gets `tabindex="-1"` and `data-jump-target` until it loses focus, and `[data-jump-target]:focus` in `index.css` paints no ring: the flash, or the heading at the top, marks the spot. `jump` says whether a click or tap made it (`{ pointer }`), and the focus then passes `focusVisible: false`. Chrome 141 ignores that option, so in Chrome the CSS rule is what hides the ring.
  - While a jump scrolls, the slider's `aria-valuenow` and `aria-valuetext` hold still (until `scrollend`, or 1.5s), so a screen reader doesn't read every place the scroll passes.

It is hidden while the Retina's footnote sheet is open (`useGeneral().superScriptActive`).

## How it is built

| Piece                                   | Role                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `src/helper/chapterTimeline.js`         | Pure model: `buildTimeline(text)` walks `useText().text` in the order TextComp renders it (intro, sections, subsections, anchored boxes after their paragraph) into items, section runs and subsections; `layoutBars` lays them out; `positionForY` maps a scroll position; `plainText` makes the excerpts. Exports `REST_H` (20) and `PEEK_H` (104).                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `src/composables/useChapterTimeline.js` | Measures each item's top once (re-measured on resize, `#text` resizing and ScrollTrigger refresh), maps the scroll to a fractional item index, builds the highlight, note and trending layers, and does `jumpTo(target, { pointer })`. The target is an item index, or `{ index, anchorId }`: the heading when it is on the page (`readingStartOf`), else the item.                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `src/components/chapter/timeline/`      | `ChapterTimeline` (the dock), `TimelineBars` (the SVG), `TimelinePreview` (hover card), `TimelineMap` (dialog, an async component). These are presentational: `jump` emits the target and `{ pointer }`. If the map fails to load, the dock lets go of its button and logs a warning. Stories are under `Chapter/ReaderShell/`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `src/helper/readerJump.js`              | Every jump in the reader: the anchor by id, then `[data-timeline-id]`, then `[data-paragraph-id]`. `jumpToElement(el, { align, flash, focus, focusVisible })` lands it `--reader-topbar-h` + 16px below the top, smoothly unless `reducedMotionPreferred()`, and can flash it (`.ob-flash`) and focus it (`focusElement`). `scrollToY` still lands a smooth jump that a ScrollTrigger refresh cut short (at `scrollend`, at most 3 tries within 3s, until the reader scrolls, clicks or types). `readingStartOf(el)` skips a section's opening transition spacer. |
| `src/views/ChapterView.vue`             | Wires the composable to the dock. It refetches trending after the reader shares or unshares a highlight, or deletes a shared one.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

Text items are found by their paragraph id. Widgets and breaks render no paragraph id, so `SectionComp`, `SubSection` and `SubSubSection` put `data-timeline-id="<paragraph id>"` on `WidgetBreakout`, `BreakImages` and `BreakSection`.

While the dock is mounted it sets `--reader-timeline-h: 20px` on `<html>`. The desktop figure pane and the opener's tool buttons (1.25rem above it) use it (`var(--reader-timeline-h, 0px)`) to stop above the dock. The floating reader sidebar keeps clear of more: while `--reader-timeline-h` is set it stays above the dock's peek height (`PEEK_H`, 104px), so the peek's map button and right end stay usable, and below the top bar, shrinking on a short window (`useDraggablePanel`'s `topInset` and `bottomInset`, drawn at `--toolkit-h`). The phone bottom sheet covers the dock.

At rest the dock is at z-index 95, under a full-screen inline figure (100). Open, it rises to 191, over the sidebar's panel (190) so the preview card isn't hidden behind it, and under the creator's edit bar (200), the map (250) and the nav drawer (300).

The creator-only Draft ribbon used to sit bottom-left, where the dock now is. It is now a badge beside the chapter number in the top bar (`ReaderTopBar`'s `isDraft`, OPENBRAIN-51). Below 400px it is an amber dot, with the word kept for screen readers.

## Data sources

- **Your highlights and notes** come from `useHighlights` and `useNotes`, which hold every chapter's rows. Only rows on this chapter's paragraphs appear.
- **Trending** comes from `trending_highlights`. Everyone, signed out included, can read the rows of published chapters, and creators can also read those of drafts. The query is chapter-scoped through the paragraph's section to its module (`fetchTrendingForModule`, at most 200 rows; `sections!section_id` names the relationship, because a bare `sections` embed is ambiguous, PGRST201). A failed request leaves the lane empty and logs `console.warn`, never `console.error`, because the CI smoke fails on console errors.
- **Widget thumbnails** are stills under `public/publicAssets/images/widgets/thumbs/`, mapped by the generated `src/widgets/thumbnails.js` (`widgetThumb(id)`). To re-capture them, start a `vite preview` of a fresh build and run `npm run widget-thumbs`. Do this when a widget changes or a new one is uploaded. Pass Supabase credentials so live uploads are read. The script header lists the flags.

## Sharing and trending

A highlight is private when it is created. In the highlight toolbar's edit mode, "Share with readers" sets `highlights.is_public`, with the help text "Counts toward Trending. Your name isn't shown". The switch moves at once. If the save fails it switches back and shows an inline alert (ChapterView answers through `done(saved)` in the `update-highlight` payload).

The switch shows only to a signed-in reader, and only once `public.trending_sharing_ready()` answers true (`useTrendingSharing`, one call per page load shared by every toolbar). That function comes with `supabase/migrations/20261007000000_trending_highlights_sync.sql`, which reaches production only through `supabase db push` (see `production-sql.md`). Before the push the call is a 404 and the switch stays hidden, because the old policy would hand a shared row to anyone. The migration:

- **Keeps highlight rows private.** It drops every SELECT policy on `highlights` except the owner one, "Users can view public highlights" included. Creators get "Creators read shared highlights" (`is_public` rows only, to moderate Trending). Other readers never see who shared.
- **Counts readers.** `trending_highlights` holds, per passage (`paragraph_id`, `start_offset`, `end_offset`), the number of distinct readers with a public highlight there whose whitespace-normalised `selected_text` occurs in the paragraph's reader text (`trending_paragraph_text`, which mirrors `contentBlocksToHTML`) or in its `content_text`. Text that isn't in the paragraph is never counted or shown. The shown text is the normalised text most of those readers chose (a tie goes to the shortest, then byte order). Triggers on insert, update and delete recount the passages they touch, under per-passage advisory locks.
- **Reads published chapters only.** A trending row is visible when its paragraph's module is published, or to a creator, so a passage shared on a draft is no more visible than the draft.
- **Rebuilds the table,** which drops drifted counts, one reader's duplicates, text that isn't in its paragraph and the `seed_dashboard_data.sql` fixture rows.

`src/__tests__/trendingHighlightsSync.sql.test.js` runs the real schema migrations and this one in PGlite, as `anon` and `authenticated`. It skips unless `HISTORY_SQL_HARNESS` is set, which the History fixture workflow does.

## Known limits

- The `NN%` label and the slider value come from `useReadingProgress`, the same document-level percentage that persistence, the dashboard and Continue Reading use. The fill is by item, so the two can differ by a few percent.
- An item whose anchor isn't on the page (not rendered yet, or a legacy `text.json` paragraph type the reader draws nothing for) takes the previous item's top. Its bar fills together with the previous one.
- Other readers' shares appear on the next chapter load. Your own appear right away.
- The sharing probe runs once per page load. After the push, readers see the switch from their next page load, and a probe that fails (offline) hides it until the next one.
- The text rule checks that a shared text occurs in its paragraph, not that it is the text at its offsets. It runs when a highlight is shared, moved or changed, so a later edit to the paragraph doesn't recount it. Text the SQL doesn't model (another HTML entity, CSS-hidden text) isn't counted.
- Only timeline and map jumps skip a transition spacer. The opener TOC, the top bar's section menu and InfoTab still land on it; they can call `jumpToElement(readingStartOf(el), …)`.
- After a click or tap closes the map, focus sits on the dock itself (not the slider), so the page's scroll keys keep working. A screen reader that only sends pointer events lands on the dock region rather than the map button.
- If the map's chunk fails to load, the next click tries again, but Chrome caches a failed module for the life of the page, so in practice it needs a reload.
- Thumbnails show each widget's first state in light mode. Three Attention markers (`upload:attn-cocktail-party`, `upload:attn-top-down-bottom-up`, `upload:attn-hemispatial-neglect`) have no uploaded HTML yet, so their cards show a hatched placeholder.
- The map's large bars are pointer-only. Keyboard and screen-reader users reach the same places through the section list.
- At rest the dock sits above full-bleed stages and full-screen figures and covers their bottom 20px.
