What you are looking at

Every pixel of this image is one model V1 neuron, and all of them share the receptive field drawn in the profile panel. So the picture is no longer an image — it is a population response map. Bright means "this neuron fired"; dark means "this neuron stayed silent".

Because the whole bank is tuned alike, only scene structure matching the preferred orientation, bar width and length survives. Hold a pen up and rotate it: it appears and vanishes as it passes through the bank's preferred orientation. That vanishing is orientation selectivity.

Each unit computes the local energy in a quadrature pair of oriented filters, so it signals where an oriented feature lies without caring which side is light and which is dark — the defining behaviour of a complex cell.

8 orientations runs eight such banks in parallel and paints each pixel with the colour of whichever bank drives it best, the same convention used for orientation preference maps in imaging experiments.

The crosshair marks one recorded neuron. Tap or drag on the image to move the electrode; its orientation tuning curve is measured live from the current frame.
